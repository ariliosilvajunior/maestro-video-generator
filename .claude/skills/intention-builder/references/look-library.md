# The look library

Thirty named looks. **Use these to propose, never to prescribe.** When a node in
Layer 4 comes up, offer two or three of these with the `Reads as` line only —
that one plain-English sentence is what a beginner can actually react to. Bring
out the spec after they have picked.

Every entry is complete enough to become a shot-builder `<STYLE-TOKEN>` without
further research: it carries reference films with DP credits, a camera mode, a
lens family, a stock, a light rule, hex palette anchors, and the measurables a
style token is required to have.

**Adapting a look:** change the palette to the project's colours, change the
location, keep the light rule and the lens family. Those two are what make it
read as the look. Change the light rule and you have picked a different look
without noticing.

**Never blend two.** Mixing two stocks or two light rules in one project is the
most common cause of footage that will not cut together.

## A · Available-light naturalism

### 1 · `revenant-available-light`
**Reads as:** Nothing is lit. The sun and the fire are the only lamps, the sky is the softbox, and faces go dark when they turn away — because that is what happens outdoors.
**Reference:** The Revenant (2015) — Emmanuel Lubezki · Children of Men (2006) — Emmanuel Lubezki
**Mode:** M1 Narrative · **Lens:** 12-21mm wide primes, close to the face, spherical
**Stock/grade:** Kodak 250D emulation, cool desaturated, blacks lifted slightly, 250 ASA fine grain
**Light rule:** Available light only. One motivated source per scene — sun, sky, fire. Never a fill you cannot point at.
**Why it works:** A wide lens held close keeps the face and the whole landscape inside the same frame, where a long-lens close-up would dissolve the trees away — the character never psychologically escapes the wilderness, and because the only light is what the day happened to give, nobody in frame looks protected.
**Palette:** `#8E9AA3` cold sky grey · `#4A5751` wet pine · `#C8B49A` breath-lit skin · `#2B2E2C` near-black forest · `#D9552F` firelight
**Measurables:** 5600K daylight / 2000K fire · contrast 6:1 · saturation 0.40 · halation 0.2 on fire only · vignette natural from the wide glass · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 48 on the lit side, 20 when a face turns away · black point 6 IRE
**It is NOT:** warm, flattering, symmetrical, or evenly exposed. Not lit from the front.
**Costs you:** You shoot when the light is there or you do not shoot. Faces will be half in shadow and clients will ask you to fix it.
**Best for:** Wilderness, survival, anything that has to feel unstaged. Long takes.

### 2 · `a24-flat-daylight`
**Reads as:** Soft window light, low contrast, nothing looks lit at all. Feels like a memory of an ordinary afternoon.
**Reference:** Moonlight (2016) — James Laxton · Aftersun (2022) — Gregory Oke · Past Lives (2023) — Shabier Kirchner
**Mode:** M1 Narrative · **Lens:** 32-50mm spherical, shot wide open, often slightly loose framing
**Stock/grade:** Low-contrast digital, milky blacks, gentle film emulation, minimal grain
**Light rule:** One large soft source, usually a window, usually behind or beside. Never a hard key.
**Why it works:** Loose framing on a near-normal lens leaves air around the subject, so the viewer sits in the room beside them instead of pressing in, and light this flat emphasises nothing — with no part of the frame being pointed at, the image stops arguing and simply lets you look.
**Palette:** `#E8DED0` bleached wall · `#9FB2B8` daylight blue · `#C89C7C` warm skin · `#5B5347` shadow ochre · `#7A9E8E` a single muted accent
**Measurables:** 5000K · contrast 3.5:1 · saturation 0.45 · lifted blacks to 8 IRE · halation 0.1 · grain 200 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 58 · black point 8 IRE
**It is NOT:** punchy, contrasty, saturated, or dramatic. No teal-orange split.
**Costs you:** It reads as small and personal. It will not sell a product or carry an action beat.
**Best for:** Intimate two-handers, coming-of-age, anything where restraint is the point.

### 3 · `magic-hour-open-country`
**Reads as:** Twenty minutes of gold, and everything in it looks like it matters. Long shadows, warm skin, a sky that does half the work.
**Reference:** Nomadland (2020) — Joshua James Richards · Days of Heaven (1978) — Néstor Almendros (Haskell Wexler shot substantial additional photography after Almendros left)
**Mode:** M1 / M5 · **Lens:** 35-85mm spherical, long end for compression against the sky
**Stock/grade:** Kodak 250D, warm-biased, deep but not crushed blacks
**Light rule:** Sun between about -4 and +6 degrees of the horizon, behind or three-quarter back. Faces get sky bounce, not sun.
**Why it works:** Backlight puts a rim between the figure and the land while the long end stacks the sky up right behind them, so a person reads as singular and small at once — the viewer sees a life at the scale of the country it happens in. Because the sun is doing the keying, the moment is visibly borrowed rather than built, and feels like something being witnessed before it goes.
**Palette:** `#E8A24B` low sun · `#B5643C` warm earth · `#7E9BB8` cool sky opposite · `#3A2E28` shadow · `#F0DCC0` rim on skin
**Measurables:** 4800K · contrast 7:1 · saturation 0.55 · halation 0.5 on the sun · grain 250 ASA · skin R:G 1.35 R:B 1.85 · skin IRE 52, the sun rim spiking well above it · black point 4 IRE
**It is NOT:** available all day. It is a twenty-minute window, twice a day.
**Costs you:** Schedule tyranny. Two takes per evening. Any pickup shot on a different day will not match.
**Best for:** Landscape, road, agriculture, anything about scale or freedom.

### 4 · `verite-handheld`
**Reads as:** A camera operator who is in the room and slightly behind on the action. Snap zooms, imperfect framing, focus caught rather than pulled.
**Reference:** Succession (2018) — Andrij Parekh / Patrick Capone / Christopher Norr · The Bear (2022) — Andrew Wehde
**Mode:** M1 Narrative · **Lens:** 24-105mm zoom, long end, operator zooming live
**Stock/grade:** 35mm Kodak Vision3 500T 5219 rated 800 ASA, no strong grade — the look is in the operating, not the grade
**Light rule:** Practicals and whatever is there. Windows blow out; that is allowed.
**Why it works:** Shooting the long end of the zoom means the camera is physically across the room reaching in, so the viewer is never inside the conversation, only catching it — and focus that arrives a beat late is the proof that nobody arranged this to be seen.
**Palette:** Whatever the location is. Do not impose one — the un-designed palette is the point.
**Measurables:** location-native white balance · contrast 5:1 · saturation 0.50 · no halation · grain 800 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 50 · black point 5 IRE
**It is NOT:** composed, stabilised, or symmetrical. Never locked off.
**Costs you:** It reads as journalism. A product will not look premium in it.
**Best for:** Dialogue under pressure, ensembles, kitchens, offices, anything that should feel overheard.

### 5 · `monochrome-formal`
**Reads as:** Black and white, wide, and patient. The camera moves sideways and lets things enter frame instead of chasing them.
**Reference:** Roma (2018) — Alfonso Cuarón · Ida (2013) — Ryszard Lenczewski / Łukasz Żal
**Mode:** M1 / M5 · **Lens:** 21-35mm, deep stop, everything sharp
**Stock/grade:** Digital monochrome, full tonal range, true blacks, no toning
**Light rule:** Ambient, high and soft. Deep focus needs stop, which needs light.
**Why it works:** Deep focus with nothing thrown soft refuses to nominate a subject, and a camera that travels sideways instead of following holds the same distance no matter who moves — the viewer has to decide for themselves what matters, which is what keeps the memory from turning sentimental.
**Palette:** None by definition. Direct the *tonal* palette instead: what is white, what is black, what is the mid-grey the film sits in.
**Measurables:** monochrome · contrast 9:1 · saturation 0.00 · grain 200 ASA fine · no halation · skin R:G/R:B n/a (monochrome) · skin IRE 52 as a grey value · black point 1 IRE
**It is NOT:** high-contrast noir, and not a colour film desaturated in post.
**Costs you:** Wardrobe and set have to be designed for tone, not colour. Half your palette decisions become value decisions.
**Best for:** Memory, period, formal distance, anything where colour would sentimentalise it.

## B · Hard-source drama

### 6 · `deakins-hard-source`
**Reads as:** One light, clearly coming from somewhere, cutting a hard edge. Enormous negative space. Nothing accidental in the frame.
**Reference:** Blade Runner 2049 (2017) — Roger Deakins · Sicario (2015) — Roger Deakins · 1917 (2019) — Roger Deakins
**Mode:** M1 / M5 · **Lens:** 27-47mm spherical, mid-stop, symmetrical framing
**Stock/grade:** Clean digital, deep true blacks, single dominant hue per scene
**Light rule:** One source, hard, obviously motivated, often from behind or above. Fill is near zero.
**Why it works:** A single hard source with near-zero fill means the viewer's eye has one place to go, so the frame feels decided rather than observed, and the symmetry plus all that empty space around the figure says the person was placed there — the viewer watches someone contained by an architecture much larger than them.
**Palette:** One dominant hue per sequence: `#D96A2B` amber wall · or `#3C6E8F` steel blue · or `#1A1A1C` black with `#E8C64A` a single practical
**Measurables:** varies by sequence, state per scene · contrast 12:1 · saturation 0.45 · halation 0.3 on practicals · grain 400 ASA · skin R:G/R:B follows the sequence hue — 1.35 / 1.90 in the amber sequences, neutral in the steel-blue ones · skin IRE 52 on the key side, 4 in the fall-off · black point 1 IRE
**It is NOT:** soft, ambient, or evenly filled. Never two competing sources.
**Costs you:** Discipline. One out-of-place lamp destroys the frame, and the framing rules mean handheld coverage will not match.
**Best for:** Thrillers, scale, interrogation, anything that needs to feel inevitable.

### 7 · `fincher-clinical`
**Reads as:** Cold, precise, slightly green, and locked down. The camera never gets excited. Faces are lit accurately and unflatteringly.
**Reference:** The Social Network (2010) — Jeff Cronenweth · Gone Girl (2014) — Jeff Cronenweth
**Mode:** M1 Narrative · **Lens:** 32-75mm spherical, mid-stop, precise headroom
**Stock/grade:** Clean digital, crushed blacks, green-cyan cast in the shadows, yellow-cast practicals
**Light rule:** Soft top-and-side sources, low fill, practicals allowed to be the wrong colour on purpose.
**Why it works:** The camera holds exact headroom and never reframes to favour whoever is talking, and the light describes faces instead of improving them — the viewer is put in the position of assessing these people rather than being invited to side with one.
**Palette:** `#1E2422` crushed green-black · `#5F6E63` cold midtone · `#C4A46A` sodium practical · `#D8D2C6` paper white · `#7E8B94` monitor blue
**Measurables:** 4300K with green tint +0.02 · contrast 11:1 · saturation 0.38 · blacks crushed to 2 IRE · grain minimal · skin R:G 1.05 R:B 1.35, the green push holding R:G down · skin IRE 50 · black point 2 IRE
**It is NOT:** warm, handheld, or forgiving. No golden hour anywhere.
**Costs you:** It reads as cold on purpose, which is wrong for anything that wants affection.
**Best for:** Procedural, corporate, tech, dread that arrives slowly.

### 8 · `sodium-street-night`
**Reads as:** City at night lit by the city itself. Orange sodium, green fluorescents, wet asphalt doing the reflecting.
**Reference:** Joker (2019) — Lawrence Sher · Drive (2011) — Newton Thomas Sigel · Taxi Driver (1976) — Michael Chapman
**Mode:** M1 Narrative · **Lens:** 40-75mm anamorphic, wide open, streak flares welcome
**Stock/grade:** Kodak 500T emulation, 800 ASA grain, deep blacks holding detail
**Light rule:** Every source is in the world — streetlamp, sign, headlight, window. No unmotivated fill, ever.
**Why it works:** Every source belongs to the street, so a character is visible only where the city permits it and falls to nothing in between — the viewer cannot hold a face continuously, and the dark between lamps stays genuinely unreadable instead of being filled in for their comfort.
**Palette:** `#E0872F` sodium orange · `#2E5E52` fluorescent green-teal · `#C4324B` sign red · `#12161C` wet black · `#8FA6B8` cold window spill
**Measurables:** mixed 2200K sodium / 4200K fluorescent · contrast 14:1 · saturation 0.60 · halation 0.6 on practicals · grain 800 ASA · skin R:G 1.55 R:B 2.60 under sodium · skin IRE 42 under the practicals · black point 3 IRE
**It is NOT:** blue-moonlight night. Nothing here is lit by the moon.
**Costs you:** Needs wet ground, needs practicals, needs a location that is already lit. Faces will go orange.
**Best for:** Loneliness in a crowd, crime, late shifts, anything walking home.

### 9 · `desert-monochrome-scale`
**Reads as:** Huge, hot, and almost colourless. One sand tone, one shadow tone, and a human being made small by both.
**Reference:** Dune (2021) — Greig Fraser · The Batman (2022) — Greig Fraser
**Mode:** M5 Atmospheric / M1 · **Lens:** 21-40mm wide, plus 135mm+ for heat compression
**Stock/grade:** Near-monochrome within a single hue family, heavy atmospheric haze, soft blacks
**Light rule:** Sun straight overhead or full backlight through dust. Bounce comes off the ground.
**Why it works:** Haze strips out the depth cues, so the land has no readable far edge, and the long-lens compression stacks a human being against that undifferentiated distance — the viewer measures the person against a space that never resolves into anywhere to go.
**Palette:** `#C9A87E` sand · `#8A7355` mid dust · `#463A2E` shadow · `#E4DCCB` haze white · one accent only, sparingly
**Measurables:** 5800K · contrast 5:1 (haze flattens it) · saturation 0.25 · halation 0.4 · grain 500 ASA · skin R:G 1.25 R:B 1.70 · skin IRE 55, the sand around it running 20 points higher · black point 10 IRE
**It is NOT:** colourful, and not sharp to the horizon. Haze is a character.
**Costs you:** Colour is almost entirely off the table, so wardrobe and props carry all the visual variety.
**Best for:** Scale, isolation, sci-fi, anything where the environment is the antagonist.

## C · Commercial and product

### 10 · `coffee-commercial-warm`
**Reads as:** Everything glows. Hard warm key raking across steam, macro on the pour, and skin that looks like it slept well.
**Reference:** The premium hot-beverage commercial register — hard warm key, backlit steam, tabletop macro
**Mode:** M2 Studio / Editorial · **Lens:** 50mm for the hands, 100mm macro for the pour and the crema
**Stock/grade:** Warm-biased, retained blacks, gentle bloom on specular highlights
**Light rule:** One hard warm key three-quarter back to catch the steam, a large soft bounce on the front, negative fill on the shadow side. Steam only reads against a dark background.
**Why it works:** The three-quarter back key exists so steam and crema become visible texture, and the macro puts the viewer nearer the pour than a drinker's own eyes ever get — the relationship being built is with the heat and the surface of the drink, not with whoever is holding it.
**Palette:** `#6B3A1E` roast brown · `#D9A24B` crema gold · `#F2E4CE` milk cream · `#2B1D14` deep shadow · `#8C5239` warm ceramic
**Measurables:** 5200K, tint +0.015 magenta · contrast 8.5:1 · saturation 0.55 · halation 0.4 on steam and specular · grain 400 ASA fine · skin R:G 1.35 R:B 1.95 · skin IRE 47 · black point 4 IRE
**It is NOT:** documentary, cool, or desaturated. Never flat front light.
**Costs you:** It is a built look, not a found one. It cannot be shot in a real café without lighting it.
**Best for:** Hot drinks, bakery, anything where warmth is the product.

### 11 · `beverage-macro-cold`
**Reads as:** Condensation, ice, and a hard edge of light down the side of the glass. Cold enough to feel on your teeth.
**Reference:** The cold-drink tabletop register — rim-lit glass, controlled condensation, high-speed pour
**Mode:** M2 Studio · **Lens:** 100mm macro, T/8 to hold the whole label
**Stock/grade:** Clean, cool-biased, high micro-contrast, crisp specular
**Light rule:** Hard rim light either side of the glass, black flags front, one small top source for the liquid. Front light kills a drink.
**Why it works:** Light touching only the edges, with the front flagged black, describes the glass as a hard cold solid rather than something glowing, and the deep stop keeps every bead of condensation sharp enough to feel — what the viewer is sold is temperature and surface, not comfort.
**Palette:** `#BFE3EE` ice cyan · `#F2F7F9` condensation white · `#1B2B33` black surround · one product colour at full saturation
**Measurables:** 6000K · contrast 13:1 · saturation 0.65 · halation 0.2 · grain none · skin R:G/R:B n/a (no skin in frame) · skin IRE n/a (no skin in frame) · black point 2 IRE
**It is NOT:** warm, ambient, or handheld. Never a locked-off shot without a slow push.
**Costs you:** Requires a tabletop stage and time. Every drop is placed.
**Best for:** Beer, soft drinks, water, anything served cold.

### 12 · `product-on-white`
**Reads as:** No world at all. The object floats in clean light and nothing distracts from its edges.
**Reference:** The consumer-electronics launch register — seamless, gradient falloff, single hero object
**Mode:** M2 Studio · **Lens:** 85-100mm, mid-stop, dead-level or a precise 15-degree offset
**Stock/grade:** Neutral, true white point, no grain, no filter
**Light rule:** Large overhead soft source, two white bounces, a gradient behind. The shadow under the object is the only anchor.
**Why it works:** Removing the world leaves nothing to read but edge, proportion and finish, and the contact shadow is the only thing telling you the object is physically real and has weight — the viewer is put in the position of appraising a thing, not visiting a place.
**Palette:** `#FFFFFF` seamless · `#EDEDED` gradient falloff · `#D6D6D6` contact shadow · product colours untouched
**Measurables:** 5600K neutral · contrast 4:1 · saturation 0.50 · no halation · no grain · nothing in frame goes darker than the contact shadow · skin R:G/R:B n/a (no skin in frame) · skin IRE n/a (no skin in frame) · black point 15 IRE
**It is NOT:** cinematic. Do not add anamorphic flare, film grain, or a teal-orange split to a product-on-white — it looks like a mistake.
**Costs you:** Zero atmosphere. All the storytelling has to happen in motion and sound.
**Best for:** Hardware, packaging, anything that must read as new.

### 13 · `beauty-soft-glow`
**Reads as:** Skin like lit porcelain. Big soft frontal source, almost no shadow, a slight bloom on every highlight.
**Reference:** The cosmetics register — beauty dish or ring source, heavy diffusion, macro on texture
**Mode:** M2 Studio · **Lens:** 85mm for face, 100mm macro for lip, lash, and skin texture
**Stock/grade:** Warm-neutral, lifted shadows, strong highlight bloom
**Light rule:** One very large source directly front and slightly above, white bounce below to erase the under-shadow. Diffusion on the lens, not just the light.
**Why it works:** A very large frontal source erases the shadows that describe bone, so the face stops being a particular structure and becomes a surface, and the macro then hands that surface over at a range no conversation permits — the viewer is looking at skin, not at a person.
**Palette:** `#F4DCD0` lit skin · `#E8B9AE` blush · `#FBF6F1` highlight bloom · `#C9A89B` soft shadow · one product colour
**Measurables:** 5400K · contrast 3:1 · saturation 0.50 · halation 0.6 · grain none · Black Pro-Mist 1/2 equivalent · skin R:G 1.22 R:B 1.50 · skin IRE 62 · black point 14 IRE
**It is NOT:** contrasty or moody. No hard shadows anywhere on the face.
**Costs you:** It flattens. Structure in a face disappears, which is the point and also the limitation.
**Best for:** Cosmetics, skincare, hair, jewellery on skin.

### 14 · `food-overhead-natural`
**Reads as:** A table by a window, shot from directly above. Real crumbs, real steam, nothing styled to death.
**Reference:** The modern food-editorial register — window-lit tabletop, top-down and 45-degree pairs
**Mode:** M2 Studio (dressed as M1) · **Lens:** 50mm top-down, 100mm macro for the 45-degree detail
**Stock/grade:** Warm-neutral, natural contrast, gentle film grain to keep it from looking sterile
**Light rule:** One large soft source from one side, at the height of a real window. Black flag opposite. The shadow direction never changes across the shoot.
**Why it works:** Top-down takes the viewer out of a diner's eye-line and puts them over the table, in the position of whoever made the food, and holding one shadow direction across the whole shoot makes every plate belong to the same real afternoon rather than to a catalogue.
**Palette:** `#E9DFCD` linen · `#8C6A45` wood · `#C4452F` a single food red · `#5E6B4E` herb green · `#3A3129` shadow
**Measurables:** 5200K · contrast 6:1 · saturation 0.55 · halation 0.2 · grain 200 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 58 (hands only) · black point 5 IRE
**It is NOT:** hard-lit or top-lit from centre. Never a ring light.
**Costs you:** Slow. Every plate is dressed and shot within a few minutes of being made.
**Best for:** Restaurants, recipes, packaging in context.

## D · Fashion and editorial

### 15 · `editorial-studio-void`
**Reads as:** A body in a colour field and nothing else. No location, no story, just silhouette, fabric and light.
**Reference:** The fashion-film register — seamless colour cyc, single sculpted source, locked frame with a slow push
**Mode:** M2 Studio / Editorial · **Lens:** Cooke-family spherical 50-100mm, T2, gentle bloom on fabric
**Stock/grade:** Saturated editorial, warm-retained blacks never crushed to zero
**Light rule:** One sculpted key, one hard rim, one controlled bounce. The background is lit separately from the subject.
**Why it works:** Lighting the background separately cuts the body out of the colour instead of placing it in a room, so there is no situation to explain what the person is doing — the viewer reads silhouette, fabric and movement as pure form, which is the only transaction a lookbook needs.
**Palette:** One field colour at full commitment plus wardrobe. `#C43A2F` red field · or `#2F4C8C` cobalt · or `#D9CBB8` bone
**Measurables:** 5400K · contrast 9:1 · saturation 0.70 · halation 0.4 on chrome and fabric sheen · grain 400 ASA · skin R:G 1.25 R:B 1.60 · skin IRE 52 · black point 6 IRE
**It is NOT:** environmental. The moment a real wall enters frame, it is a different look.
**Costs you:** No world means no context — the clothes and the body have to carry everything.
**Best for:** Lookbooks, fashion film, dance, performance-on-set, music video editorial sequences.

### 16 · `hard-flash-editorial`
**Reads as:** Direct on-camera flash. Harsh, unglamorous, immediate — like a photograph someone took of you without asking.
**Reference:** The on-camera-flash editorial register — direct frontal strobe, hard falloff, black background
**Mode:** M2 Studio · **Lens:** 28-50mm, close, slight distortion accepted as character
**Stock/grade:** High-contrast, cool-neutral, hard specular on skin, deep black falloff
**Light rule:** Source at the lens axis. Background goes black by distance, not by flagging.
**Why it works:** The source sits on the lens axis, so the subject is lit by the very act of being looked at, and the background dies off by distance — nothing exists except what the viewer's own light reached, which makes the viewer the intruder rather than a witness.
**Palette:** `#F0EDE8` blown highlight · `#B08E7E` hard-lit skin · `#000000` falloff · saturated wardrobe reads at full strength
**Measurables:** 5600K · contrast 16:1 · saturation 0.65 · no halation · grain 800 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral, biased cool · skin IRE 55-75 depending on how square the face is to the flash · black point 0 IRE
**It is NOT:** flattering, soft, or warm.
**Costs you:** It reads as youth-coded and slightly aggressive. Wrong for luxury, right for streetwear.
**Best for:** Street fashion, music, party coverage, anything with attitude.

### 17 · `window-lookbook`
**Reads as:** Daylight through a big window in an empty apartment. Soft, quiet, expensive without trying.
**Reference:** The daylight-interior lookbook register — north-facing window, bare walls, minimal styling
**Mode:** M2 / M1 · **Lens:** 35-85mm spherical, wide open
**Stock/grade:** Neutral-warm, low contrast, film-like roll-off in the highlights
**Light rule:** One window, subject turned into it or three-quarter to it. White bounce only. Never a second source.
**Why it works:** One window and nothing else means the room's emptiness is the entire set, so attention has only the light, the wall and the clothes to spend itself on — and with the subject turned into the window, the viewer stands on the shadow side of them, close by but not addressed.
**Palette:** `#EFE9E0` wall · `#C7B39C` wood floor · `#D8C4B4` skin in daylight · `#6E7A80` window blue · wardrobe carries any accent
**Measurables:** 5600K · contrast 5:1 · saturation 0.45 · halation 0.2 · grain 200 ASA · skin R:G 1.20 R:B 1.45, barely warm · skin IRE 56 · black point 7 IRE
**It is NOT:** studio-lit. If you can see a lamp's shadow, it is broken.
**Costs you:** Time of day dictates the shoot, and the room has to be genuinely empty.
**Best for:** Apparel, interiors, quiet brand films, portraits.

## E · Music video and performance

### 18 · `neon-intimacy`
**Reads as:** Saturated coloured light on skin at close range. Two colours fighting across one face, camera almost touching.
**Reference:** Euphoria (2019-2022) — Marcell Rév (pilot and majority) · Enter the Void (2009) — Benoît Debie
**Mode:** M1 / M4 · **Lens:** 25-50mm wide open, very close, heavy diffusion
**Stock/grade:** Lifted blacks with colour in them, strong bloom — season 1 (2019) is digital, Alexa 65; the film look, Kodak Vision3 500T with roughly half the season on Ektachrome 100D, is season 2 (2022)
**Light rule:** Two coloured practicals in opposition, one warm one cold, both in frame or clearly just out of it. Skin is allowed to take the colour.
**Why it works:** A wide lens at touching distance puts the viewer inside the subject's personal space, nearer than any stranger stands, while two opposed colours split the face so it never resolves into one readable expression — closeness without comprehension.
**Palette:** `#D42A6B` magenta · `#2BA8D4` cyan · `#7A2FA8` violet mix · `#0E0A14` black with colour lift · `#E8C8B0` skin holding through
**Measurables:** mixed 3200K/6500K · contrast 10:1 · saturation 0.75 · halation 0.7 · grain 800 ASA · Black Pro-Mist 1/2 · skin R:G/R:B abandoned on purpose — magenta side 1.90 / 1.00, cyan side 0.80 / 0.70 · skin IRE 48 on whichever colour is keying · black point 9 IRE
**It is NOT:** naturalistic. Nobody's house looks like this.
**Costs you:** Skin tone accuracy. Faces will not be their real colour, and that is non-negotiable in this look.
**Best for:** Music video, nightlife, altered states, youth drama.

### 19 · `concert-pit`
**Reads as:** Shot from the photo pit by someone being shoved. Stage lights doing whatever they want, haze in every beam.
**Reference:** The concert-documentary register — pit-photographer handheld, orbital passes, hard cuts on the beat
**Mode:** M4 Performance · **Lens:** 40-100mm anamorphic, wide open, flares embraced
**Stock/grade:** Kodak 250D, slightly desaturated cool with warm highlight bloom, deep blacks holding detail
**Light rule:** Stage lighting is the only lighting. Haze is mandatory — without it there are no beams and the look does not exist.
**Why it works:** Haze turns the beams into solid objects filling the space between camera and stage, so the viewer is always looking through the room at the performer rather than straight at them, and the pit-level shoving keeps that vantage a body in a crowd instead of a seat.
**Palette:** Take the stage cast and commit: `#C42A5B` magenta wash · `#F0A83C` amber side · `#2ED0E0` cyan strobe · `#14121A` pit black
**Measurables:** stage-native mixed · contrast 15:1 · saturation 0.55 · halation 0.6 · grain 800 ASA · skin R:G/R:B stage-dictated, no fixed target · skin IRE 50 when a beam finds the face, 10 when it does not · black point 3 IRE
**It is NOT:** stabilised or composed. No locked-off shots anywhere.
**Costs you:** You cannot control anything. Coverage is a matter of luck and how many cameras you have.
**Best for:** Live music, crowds, festivals, sports arenas.

### 20 · `90s-video`
**Reads as:** Video, not film. Slight smear on motion, blown highlights, over-saturated, a hint of tape.
**Reference:** The 90s music-video register — video-origination look, snap zooms, in-camera transitions
**Mode:** M4 / M2 · **Lens:** Zoom, operated live, snap-zoom moves
**Stock/grade:** Video gamma, blown highlights that do not roll off, chroma bleed on reds
**Light rule:** Hard, frontal, unsubtle. Coloured gels straight onto the background.
**Why it works:** Hard frontal light and highlights that clip instead of rolling off flatten everything onto a single plane, so the image reads as a broadcast surface rather than a space you could walk into, and the live snap zoom means the framing is being performed at the viewer in real time.
**Palette:** `#E8322F` bleeding red · `#2F5FE8` electric blue · `#F2E33C` yellow · `#FFFFFF` clipped white · `#101010` video black
**Measurables:** 3200K tungsten-native · contrast 8:1 clipped at the top · saturation 0.85 · chroma bleed on saturated reds · no film grain, tape noise instead · video setup at the bottom, not zero · skin R:G 1.30 R:B 1.55 with the red bleeding · skin IRE 58, the specular hits clipping straight to 100 · black point 7.5 IRE
**It is NOT:** filmic. Do not add grain or halation — that undoes it.
**Costs you:** It is a period signal. It will read as a reference to the 90s, never as neutral.
**Best for:** Music video, fashion with irony, anything nostalgic about broadcast.

### 21 · `one-take-performance`
**Reads as:** A single unbroken move around a performer. No cuts to hide behind, so the performance has to be real.
**Reference:** The single-take performance register — steadicam or gimbal orbit, one continuous lighting state
**Mode:** M2 / M4 · **Lens:** 24-35mm, deep enough stop to hold focus through the move
**Stock/grade:** Whatever the paired look is — the take is the structure, not the grade
**Light rule:** 360-degree lighting. Every direction the camera can point has to work, which usually means overhead soft plus in-frame practicals.
**Why it works:** With no cut, the viewer's time and the performer's time are the same unbroken stretch, so nothing could have been repaired between two frames — attention shifts from what is happening to whether they can hold it, which is how the take itself becomes the drama.
**Palette:** Inherit from the paired look.
**Measurables:** Inherit. Add: one continuous exposure — no ramping mid-take · skin R:G/R:B inherit · skin IRE inherit · black point inherit — all three locked for the length of the take.
**It is NOT:** coverage. There is no safety.
**Costs you:** Everything has to work at once, and a bad take is a whole take.
**Best for:** Dance, music performance, a single monologue, a process shown end to end.

## F · Period and stock character

### 22 · `kodachrome-70s`
**Reads as:** Slide film from 1974. Warm, dense, red-forward, blacks that go properly black.
**Reference:** The Kodachrome still register · The Master (2012) — Mihai Mălaimare Jr.
**Mode:** M1 · **Lens:** 35-85mm spherical vintage, mild uncorrected aberration
**Stock/grade:** Kodachrome 64 emulation: dense saturated reds, cool-neutral highlights, hard black point
**Light rule:** Sunlight or tungsten, not mixed. Period practicals are warm and dim.
**Why it works:** Dense reds sitting on a hard black point make the frame read as a physical slide rather than a window, so the viewer's relationship is to a record of a moment instead of the moment — the past arrives already fixed and out of reach.
**Palette:** `#B8322A` dense red · `#C89A3C` warm ochre · `#2F5B4C` bottle green · `#E8DCC4` aged paper · `#161310` true black
**Measurables:** 5500K daylight / 3200K tungsten, never mixed · contrast 10:1 · saturation 0.70 with reds pushed · halation 0.3 · grain 200 ASA tight · skin R:G 1.40 R:B 1.85 · skin IRE 50 · black point 2 IRE
**It is NOT:** faded or washed out. Kodachrome did not fade — that is Ektachrome.
**Costs you:** Wardrobe and production design have to hold up, because the palette will make period errors obvious.
**Best for:** Period drama, archival pastiche, brands with heritage claims.

### 23 · `super8-memory`
**Reads as:** Home movie. Soft, jittery in the gate, blown corners, colours that have shifted with age.
**Reference:** The Super 8 register — small-gauge grain, gate weave, usually silent
**Mode:** M1 · **Lens:** Fixed wide, no focus pull, everything roughly sharp
**Stock/grade:** Heavy grain, soft corners, faded cyan-shifted blacks, warm centre
**Light rule:** Daylight, mostly. Interiors are underexposed and that is correct.
**Why it works:** Nothing is pulled into focus and the corners fall apart, so nobody is being pointed at — this is a record someone grabbed, not a shot someone composed, and the gate weave keeps a hand and a machine present between the viewer and the memory.
**Palette:** `#D9B48C` faded warm · `#8AA39B` shifted cyan shadow · `#E8D2A8` blown highlight · `#4A4038` muddy black
**Measurables:** daylight-native · contrast 5:1 with milky blacks at 12 IRE · saturation 0.40 shifted warm · gate weave 1-2px · grain very heavy · skin R:G 1.30 R:B 1.75 · skin IRE 55 in daylight, 30 in the interiors this look underexposes on purpose · black point 12 IRE
**It is NOT:** a filter on sharp footage. The softness, the weave and the frame rate all have to move together.
**Costs you:** Detail. Nothing in this look is legible at small sizes.
**Best for:** Memory sequences, flashbacks, brand nostalgia, anything about childhood.

### 24 · `bleach-bypass-conflict`
**Reads as:** Colour drained out but contrast pushed up. Silver, harsh, grainy — the frame looks like it has been through something.
**Reference:** Saving Private Ryan (1998) — Janusz Kamiński · Se7en (1995) — Darius Khondji
**Mode:** M3 Action / Combat · **Lens:** 40-75mm, shutter angle reduced to 45-90 degrees for the staccato motion
**Stock/grade:** Bleach-bypass emulation — retained silver, crushed saturation, heavy grain
**Light rule:** Overcast or hard sun, no fill, no glamour. Practical smoke and dust in the air.
**Why it works:** Draining the colour removes the cue that separates a person from mud and metal, so bodies read as the same material as the ground, and the reduced shutter angle breaks motion into steps the eye cannot glide over — nothing in the frame lets the viewer settle into it.
**Palette:** `#8A8E88` silver grey · `#5B5A4E` olive · `#3A302A` mud · `#C4C0B4` blown sky · `#7A2A22` the only red that matters
**Measurables:** 5600K · contrast 16:1 · saturation 0.20 · halation 0.3 · grain 800 ASA heavy · shutter 45-90 degrees · skin R:G/R:B neutral — there is not enough saturation left to carry a bias · skin IRE 48 · black point 2 IRE
**It is NOT:** teal-orange. There is almost no colour to split.
**Costs you:** Skin looks bad, which is the intent, and it dates the film to a specific late-90s vocabulary.
**Best for:** Conflict, disaster, industrial, anything with dust and consequence.

### 25 · `saturated-longing`
**Reads as:** Deep greens and reds, slow motion, faces half hidden by foreground. Everything is looked at through something.
**Reference:** In the Mood for Love (2000) — Christopher Doyle / Mark Lee Ping-bing · Chungking Express (1994) — Christopher Doyle / Andrew Lau Wai-keung
**Mode:** M1 · **Lens:** 50-85mm, shot through doorways, curtains, glass, foreground clutter
**Stock/grade:** Kodak 500T, dense saturation, warm practicals against cool ambience
**Light rule:** Practical lamps, deliberately dim, plus one warm source outside a window. Framing device in front of the lens, always.
**Why it works:** Something is always between the lens and the subject, which puts the viewer in a doorway looking in rather than in the room, and the deliberately dim practicals keep faces low enough that you have to lean toward them — the look builds wanting out of the fact that access is refused.
**Palette:** `#8C1F2A` deep red · `#2E4A3C` bottle green · `#C99A4B` lamp gold · `#1A1418` warm black · `#D9B8A0` skin in tungsten
**Measurables:** 3200K tungsten · contrast 11:1 · saturation 0.75 · halation 0.5 · grain 500 ASA · skin R:G 1.40 R:B 2.00 · skin IRE 44, the practicals being deliberately dim · black point 3 IRE
**It is NOT:** clean or open. If nothing is between the lens and the subject, it is not this look.
**Costs you:** Coverage is slow and the framing devices have to exist in the location.
**Best for:** Longing, restraint, period romance, anything about not saying it.

## G · Stylised and genre

### 26 · `symmetry-pastel`
**Reads as:** Dead-centre framing, flat frontal light, a pastel palette locked to three colours, and a camera that only moves on axes.
**Reference:** The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014) — Robert Yeoman · Asteroid City (2023) — Robert Yeoman
**Mode:** M2 Studio · **Lens:** 27-40mm, planar, everything parallel to the sensor
**Stock/grade:** Even, bright, low-contrast, saturated in pastel range
**Light rule:** Flat and frontal. Shadows are the enemy of the joke.
**Why it works:** Dead-centre framing and shadowless light make it plain that a person arranged every object in the frame, so the viewer watches a made thing rather than believing in a world, and because the subject never leaves the middle, cuts land like beats — the artifice is the joke the viewer is let in on.
**Palette:** Commit to three, no more: `#E8A0A8` pink · `#D9C46A` mustard · `#7FA8B8` powder blue · plus `#F2EDE4` cream ground
**Measurables:** 5600K · contrast 4:1 · saturation 0.65 in pastel range only · no halation · grain 200 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 60 · black point 12 IRE
**It is NOT:** naturalistic in any respect. Handheld destroys it instantly.
**Costs you:** Production design becomes the budget. Every object in frame has to be chosen.
**Best for:** Comedy, whimsy, brand worlds, anything that benefits from artifice being visible.

### 27 · `underexposed-dread`
**Reads as:** Too dark. Something is in the shadow and the camera refuses to look at it directly. Wide, still, and patient.
**Reference:** Hereditary (2018) — Pawel Pogorzelski · The Killing of a Sacred Deer (2017) — Thimios Bakatakis · (The Lighthouse (2019) — Jarin Blaschke is the monochrome cousin of this look — Kodak Double-X 5222, 1.19:1 — and cannot carry the palette below)
**Mode:** M1 / M5 · **Lens:** 21-35mm, locked off, subject small in frame
**Stock/grade:** Underexposed by a stop, blacks that hold just enough, cool-neutral
**Light rule:** One dim practical, motivated, usually behind the subject. The rest of the room is genuinely dark.
**Why it works:** A locked-off wide with the subject small leaves large areas of the frame unresolved, and the camera refuses to go over and check them, so the viewer ends up searching the shadows themselves and never gets confirmation — the dread is manufactured by what the frame withholds, not by anything shown.
**Palette:** `#1A1D1C` near black · `#3E4740` shadow green · `#8A7A5E` the single practical · `#C4BCA8` a face barely lit
**Measurables:** 3800K · contrast 18:1 · saturation 0.30 · halation 0.2 · grain 800 ASA · skin R:G/R:B neutral · skin IRE 32 · black point 3 IRE
**It is NOT:** a jump-scare look. It never moves fast.
**Costs you:** It will look broken on a phone in daylight. Delivery has to be controlled.
**Best for:** Horror, dread, grief, anything where waiting is the point.

### 28 · `oversaturated-chase`
**Reads as:** Blazing orange desert, electric blue night, everything centred and sped up. Colour turned past what is comfortable.
**Reference:** Mad Max: Fury Road (2015) — John Seale
**Mode:** M3 Action / Combat · **Lens:** 24-50mm, subject centre-framed so cuts land without eye travel
**Stock/grade:** Extreme grade split — day pushed orange, night pushed blue, both past naturalism
**Light rule:** Hard sun by day, hard blue moonlight-look by night. No in-between states.
**Why it works:** Centre-framing means the eye never has to travel across a cut, so shots can arrive faster than a viewer could otherwise track, and the two-state grade says where you are in a single frame — the whole look exists to keep an audience oriented at a speed that should have lost them.
**Palette:** `#E8722A` day orange · `#B84A18` rust · `#2A5CC4` night blue · `#0E1A38` deep night · `#F2D8A8` blown sand highlight
**Measurables:** day 4000K pushed warm / night 8000K pushed cool · contrast 12:1 · saturation 0.85 · halation 0.5 · grain 400 ASA · skin R:G 1.45 R:B 2.10 by day, inverted at night to 0.80 / 0.65 · skin IRE 52 day, 32 night · black point 3 IRE
**It is NOT:** subtle. Do not use it if anything in the film needs to feel real.
**Costs you:** Skin tones go, and the two-state grade means day and night are effectively two looks that must not meet in a dissolve.
**Best for:** Chase, vehicles, high-velocity action, anything shot in a desert.

### 29 · `aerial-daylight-action`
**Reads as:** Bright, clean, high-altitude daylight. Real machinery, real G-force, and a camera bolted to something that is moving fast.
**Reference:** Top Gun: Maverick (2022) — Claudio Miranda
**Mode:** M3 Action · **Lens:** 16-35mm mounted, plus 100-300mm for air-to-air compression
**Stock/grade:** Clean, bright, mild warm bias, blue sky held at full saturation without going electric
**Light rule:** Full sun at altitude. Fill comes off the airframe. Nothing is added.
**Why it works:** The mounted wide puts the viewer in the seat with the horizon swinging around a real face, then the long air-to-air pulls back to show that same aircraft as one small object in real sky — the alternation is what proves it actually happened, and the brightness refuses to hide any of the work.
**Palette:** `#2F6FB8` sky blue · `#D9DEE2` aluminium · `#E8B23C` sun on skin · `#1E2A33` cockpit shadow · `#C4402F` warning red
**Measurables:** 5600K · contrast 8:1 · saturation 0.60 · halation 0.3 on canopy glass · grain 200 ASA · skin R:G 1.22 R:B 1.55 · skin IRE 55, dropping to 40 when the canopy shades the face · black point 4 IRE
**It is NOT:** graded dark or gritty. This look is legible and bright on purpose.
**Costs you:** Mounted cameras and real vehicles, or it reads as a video game.
**Best for:** Aviation, motorsport, sports, machinery, anything about capability.

### 30 · `hypersaturated-storybook`
**Reads as:** More colour than the world has. Wide lenses close to faces, warm-gold everywhere, a slightly unreal charm.
**Reference:** Amélie (2001) — Bruno Delbonnel · Poor Things (2023) — Robbie Ryan
**Mode:** M1 / M2 · **Lens:** 14-28mm wide primes used close, distortion embraced
**Stock/grade:** Golden-green push, saturated, warm highlights, greens pulled toward the gold
**Light rule:** Warm practicals plus a warm key. Every source is gelled toward gold.
**Why it works:** Wide glass used close enlarges the features and bends the room around them, so a person becomes a character rather than a documented human, and gelling every source toward gold means no light in the film even pretends to be neutral — the viewer accepts from the first frame that this world is being told to them, not observed.
**Palette:** `#C4451F` signature red · `#3E7A3C` pushed green · `#D9A02A` gold wash · `#2A1A12` warm black · `#E8C8A0` skin
**Measurables:** 3600K pushed warm · contrast 7:1 · saturation 0.80 with green-gold cross-process · halation 0.5 · grain 400 ASA · skin R:G 1.45 R:B 2.10 · skin IRE 56 · black point 5 IRE
**It is NOT:** neutral. There is no such thing as a neutral frame in this look.
**Costs you:** Nothing in it will read as documentary or serious. It announces itself.
**Best for:** Whimsy, fable, character comedy, brands with a storybook voice.

## Picking fast

When the user cannot choose, ask one of these instead of naming looks:

| Their answer | Points to |
|---|---|
| "It should feel real / like it actually happened" | 1, 2, 4 |
| "It should feel expensive" | 6, 10, 15, 17 |
| "It should feel cold / clinical / corporate" | 7, 12 |
| "It should feel dangerous" | 8, 24, 27 |
| "It should make you want the thing" | 10, 11, 13, 14 |
| "It should feel young / loud" | 16, 18, 20 |
| "It should feel like a memory" | 2, 5, 23, 25 |
| "It should feel big" | 3, 6, 9, 29 |
| "It should feel funny / designed / deliberate" | 26, 30 |
| "It should feel like a music video" | 15, 18, 19, 20, 21 |

And when they name a film you do not have here: find the DP, find their light
rule and their lens family, write the entry on the spot in this same shape, and
put it in the doc. The library is a starting set, not a closed list.
