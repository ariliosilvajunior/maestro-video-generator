# Vague to spec

The words below are the ones beginners reach for when they have a feeling and no
vocabulary. They are not useless — each one *does* mean something, it just means
three or four incompatible things at once. Treated as answers, they produce a
brief that fails at generation time. Treated as *questions*, they are the fastest
route into a real decision.

**The move, every time:** do not correct the word, and do not ask what they mean
by it. Offer the two or three concrete things it usually means, in plain
language, and let them point.

```
You said "cinematic." That word covers about four different things — which one
is closest?
  a) The background falls away and only the face is sharp
  b) Wide, letterboxed, the room is huge and they are small in it
  c) Warm-and-teal colour, hard shadows, everything looks graded
  d) The camera barely moves and holds longer than feels comfortable
➡️ (b), from what you have described. Costs you: wide frames need the location
to be genuinely good, because you will see all of it.
```

Never present more than four options. Never make them abstract. "The background
falls away" beats "shallow depth of field" every time, and you can name the
technical term afterward once they have chosen.

## The table

| They say | It usually means one of these | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **cinematic** | shallow focus · widescreen letterbox · graded colour · slow deliberate camera | Which of these four is closest? |
| **epic** | scale (subject small in a big frame) · slow motion · a score that swells · aerial or crane movement | Big frames, or slow time? |
| **clean** | white or empty background · no clutter in frame · sharp and bright · minimal graphics | Empty room, or bright and sharp? |
| **moody** | underexposed · one hard source · cool colour · slow pace | Dark, or just cold? |
| **vibey** | coloured practical light · handheld · music-led cutting · a party or night setting | Is the vibe in the light or in the edit? |
| **aesthetic** | a consistent limited palette · symmetry · soft light · film grain | Which one thing must be true in every frame? |
| **premium** | controlled light with clear falloff · slow camera · restrained palette · macro detail on materials | Expensive-looking usually means slow and dark. Agreed? |
| **high-end** | same as premium, plus: no visible compromise — no cheap locations, no clutter | What is the one thing in frame that would give it away as cheap? |
| **raw** | handheld · available light · no colour grade · imperfect framing | Unlit, or unstable? |
| **gritty** | desaturated · heavy grain · hard light · dirt and texture in frame | Is the grit in the grade or in the location? |
| **dreamy** | soft diffusion and bloom · slow motion · overexposed highlights · warm | Soft and bright, or slow? |
| **modern** | flat lighting · neutral colour · clean type · wide lens | Modern usually means *less*. What comes out? |
| **authentic** | non-actors · available light · handheld · unstyled wardrobe | Which are you willing to give up — control, or polish? |
| **professional** | usually just: lit, stable, and colour-consistent | This one is a baseline, not a look. Which of the real looks do you want on top? |
| **artsy** | unusual framing · long takes · no explanation · non-realistic colour | Are we hiding information from the viewer on purpose? |
| **warm** | orange-biased grade · tungsten practicals · golden hour · skin pushed red | Is the warmth in the lamps or in the grade? |
| **dark** | underexposed · night · crushed blacks · heavy subject matter | Dark image, or dark story? They are unrelated. |
| **minimal** | few objects · few colours · few cuts · negative space | Fewer things, or fewer colours? |
| **dynamic** | camera movement · fast cutting · movement inside the frame · angle variety | Is the movement the camera's or the subject's? |
| **film look** | grain · halation on highlights · softer highlight roll-off · a specific stock | Which decade of film? That is the actual question. |
| **natural** | available light · no grade · neutral colour · no camera movement | Natural light, or natural performance? |
| **editorial** | studio · one strong source · saturated · locked frame | Fashion-editorial, or magazine-documentary? |
| **viral** | strong first frame · one clear idea · works muted · a hook in the first second | This is a structure request, not a look. Let us do structure. |
| **storytelling** | a beginning and an ending · a change in someone · a reason to keep watching | Who changes, and how do we see it? |
| **luxury** | slow · dark · macro on materials · almost no cuts · silence | Luxury is usually restraint. What are we removing? |
| **fun** | bright · saturated · fast cuts · faces · music-led | Fun in the world, or fun in the edit? |
| **nostalgic** | a specific format (Super 8, VHS, Kodachrome) · warm faded grade · soft focus | Which year are we pretending it is? |
| **futuristic** | cool blue-cyan · practical LED sources · glass and reflection · clean geometry | Optimistic future or dirty future? They look nothing alike. |
| **sexy** | low key · rim light · skin texture · slow · close | Suggestive framing or suggestive lighting? |
| **cozy** | warm practicals · soft shadow · shallow focus · texture (wood, wool, steam) | What is the light source in the room? |
| **make it pop** | more contrast · more saturation · a stronger key · a colour accent | Something needs to be brighter than everything else. What? |
| **something like [brand]** | their palette · their type · their tone of voice · their pace | Which of those four are we actually borrowing? |

## Words that are not vague, and should be taken at face value

Do not run this process on these. They are specific, and treating them as vague
insults the user:

`handheld` · `locked off` · `black and white` · `slow motion` · `one take` ·
`vertical` · `close-up` · `wide` · `night` · `golden hour` · `no music` ·
`no dialogue` · a named film · a named DP · a named stock · a hex code

---

## The reverse move: when they *only* give references

Some users have the opposite problem — they name six films and no intention. The
job flips: find what the references share, name it, and check.

```
Your three references share exactly one thing: none of them has a single
close-up until past the halfway point. Everything else about them is
different — the colour, the pace, the period.

So I think what you actually want is *distance*, and the rest is negotiable.
➡️ Lock: no close-up before the midpoint. Then we choose the palette freely.
```

This is often the most valuable single sentence in a session. References carry an
intention the user has not consciously extracted — extract it, say it back, and
watch them recognise it.

If the references share nothing, say that too: *"These three do not have a common
look. Pick which one is the actual target and I will treat the other two as
things you like, not things we are making."*
